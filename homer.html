The Odyssey

A Journey of Ten Years

by Homer

✣ ✣ ✣

Book I: Invocation and Odysseus

Sing, O goddess, of the man of many wiles, who wandered far and wide after he had sacked the holy citadel of Troy. Many were the men whose cities he saw, and whose minds he learned, and many were the sorrows he suffered in his heart upon the sea, striving to win the lives of his comrades and their return home.
Yet even so he saved not his comrades, though he strove sore; for through their own blind folly they perished, fools that devoured the cattle of Helios Hyperion. But the god reft from them the day of their return. Of these things, O goddess, daughter of Zeus, tell thou to us, beginning where thou wilt.
Then all the other heroes, who had escaped sheer destruction, were now at home, safe from the perils of war and sea. But Odysseus alone, yearning to reach his home and his wife, was kept by the powerful nymph Calypso in her hollow cave, longing to be his wife.

The Hero's Journey

Ten years had passed since Odysseus and his men sailed from Troy. Ten years of battle, suffering, and wandering through unknown seas. Though his body might wander in distant lands, his heart remained fixed upon Ithaca, his beloved home.
Through trials of terrible monsters and enchanted shores, through the wrath of angry gods and the seductions of immortal maidens, did noble Odysseus persist. His cunning mind and brave spirit never wavered, though the path home seemed ever more distant.
In every peril, whether facing the Cyclops with his single gleaming eye, or navigating the straits between Scylla and Charybdis, Odysseus endured. For what greater calling could a man have than to return to his home, to his faithful wife Penelope, and to reclaim his rightful throne?
Thus sings the tale of Odysseus, whose name shall echo through the ages. A hero of Greece, whose cunning and courage transformed suffering into wisdom, and exile into the greatest homecoming tale ever told.
